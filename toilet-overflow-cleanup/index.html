Toilet Overflow Cleanup in San Bernardino: What to Know

A toilet overflow looks like a mop job, but the water does not stay on the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. What matters most is what was in it. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

In San Bernardino, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. With clean water, the job is extraction followed by drying. Gray or black water means protective gear, removal of soaked porous materials like carpet pad, and disinfection of every surface it touched. Then we check the subfloor, the wall bases and the room underneath with moisture meters so nothing is left wet.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the WaterTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainNo more flushing, and no running the washer, a shower or a sink. A blockage in the drain line means water sent down any fixture can back up.
  3. Avoid Dirty WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathWith a second floor bathroom, inspect the ceiling of the room beneath. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Block the SpreadLay down old towels so clean water does not travel into hallways or onto carpet. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Bowl water pushed over the rim by a clog in the drain line or the trap.
  • Fill valves or flappers that stick, so the tank never stops running.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
  • A child's toy, wipes or a wad of paper that gets flushed and sticks in the trap.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Lingering Odor

Contaminated water that dries inside a wall base or under flooring leaves a smell that returns in humid weather until the source material is taken out.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

Sitting at floor level, door casings and baseboards wick water up from their lower edge. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Ceiling Below

From a second floor bathroom, water follows the drain pipe down and pools on the ceiling drywall beneath, leaving stains, sagging and sometimes a collapse.

Bacterial Contamination

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

Insurance

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.

Our Process

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Identify the Category

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extraction and Removal

Our crew removes the standing water, takes out any baseboard, drywall or carpet pad that is contaminated, and pulls the toilet if water got below its base and the flange.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. When the water is Category 3, we finish this step before turning on any drying equipment.

4

Dry and Verify

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the bathroom and any affected room below. Moisture readings continue until the ceiling and subfloor return to normal levels.
