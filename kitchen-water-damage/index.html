Kitchen Water Damage in San Bernardino

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in San Bernardino, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Warning Signs

  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

Behind the Dishwasher

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.
