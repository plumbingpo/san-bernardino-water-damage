Drying a home is only half the job. After readings return to normal, the bare subfloor, cut-out drywall and missing floors still need attention. Rebuilding all of that is what water damage repair means. For San Bernardino homeowners, our crew installs replacements for anything unsalvageable and blends them with the existing finishes until the space stops looking like a job site.

Repair work covers a wide range. Smaller projects might involve only paint, fresh baseboard and a short section of drywall. Large jobs can include insulation, whole drywall sheets, subfloor, laminate or hardwood, cabinet boxes, doors and trim. We replace materials like for like where possible, and we tell you plainly when an exact match is not available so you can choose.

Dry first, then cover: that order never changes. A moisture meter has to show normal levels in the subfloor and framing before new material is installed. Mold hidden in a newly painted wall usually traces back to someone covering damp lumber. You also receive a written scope and schedule ahead of the work, which spells out what gets rebuilt and when.

When to Call for Water Damage Repair in San Bernardino

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Tiles sound hollow, have come loose or show cracked grout since water got beneath them.
  • Seams are opening, or edges are curling and swelling, on vinyl plank or laminate.
  • A door frame or slab swelled, so the door rubs or fails to close.
  • Ceiling drywall is sagging, cracked or crumbling after a leak from above.
  • Flooring flexes more than it should in the spot that was flooded.
  • You see brown stains, peeling or bubbles in paint along the path the water took.
  • Cabinet bases or side panels are swollen, soft or peeling at the bottom.

How We Handle Water Damage Repair

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

1

Confirm It Is Dry

Final moisture readings are taken on the subfloor, framing and any drywall left in place. Reconstruction only begins once those numbers are at normal levels for the material, so nothing damp gets sealed in.

2

Put the Scope in Writing

Each room is itemized in a document showing replacements, the materials chosen and how long we expect it to take. When there is an open claim, the scope is coordinated with your adjuster.

3

Reconstruct and Install

We refill open cavities with insulation, hang, tape and finish drywall, and repair the subfloor. After that we install trim, cabinetry and floors, ordering the work so completed surfaces are not damaged.

4

Finish and Walk Through

Texture is matched, walls are primed and painted, and baseboards, doors and hardware go back on. Then we walk every room with you and fix anything on the list before we call the job complete.

Why Hire a Professional for Water Damage Repair?

This is not the same as a standard renovation project. The person rebuilding needs to know what the water did, where it went and whether the structure behind the wall is truly dry. Because our crew has the drying-phase moisture logs, it knows where studs were wet, what subfloor received treatment and which areas deserve another check. With that history in hand, problems do not get buried under fresh paint and new drywall.

How to Prevent Water Damage Repair Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.
  • Fix the source first, and only then patch or paint a water stain.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • Use moisture resistant drywall and backer board when rebuilding bathrooms, kitchens and laundry rooms.
  • File the written scope and photos of the completed repair with your household papers.
  • Future repairs match better when you have held on to extra flooring, tile and the paint information.
